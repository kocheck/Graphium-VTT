Privacy Policy

Graphium for macOS. Last updated November 23, 2026.

The short version

Graphium collects no data. It has no accounts, analytics, advertising, tracking or crash upload, and its developer never receives your campaigns. Your maps, tokens, notes and sounds stay in files on your Mac that you own. Graphium only uses the network when you ask it to, and the one way campaign content can reach another company is through an AI app you connect yourself, described below.

Two editions

Graphium comes from the Mac App Store and as a direct download. Both work the same way offline. Where they differ, this page says so: the App Store edition can let AI apps use its map tools; the direct download can too, and also has an optional Agent window that chats with Claude Code or Codex inside Graphium.

Using Graphium offline

Everything you do to build and run a game works with no network: campaigns, maps, tokens, fog, lighting (the depth model for Relief Lighting is built into the app), sounds, the World View on your TV or projector, Quick Look previews and Spotlight. A campaign is a file you choose where to save, and Graphium reads and writes it on your Mac only. Sound folders you add are played where they are and never uploaded. Spotlight indexes only a campaign's name and how many maps it has, on your Mac, never names or notes from inside it.

When Graphium uses the network

Graphium contacts another computer only when you ask it to:

  • Board pack downloads. If a Session Console pack you import lists files on the web, Graphium names those sites, with any unusual port, and asks first. It downloads the files only if you choose Download, only from public addresses, and without cookies or passwords. Those sites see an ordinary web request from your Mac, as they would from a browser. Nothing from your campaign is sent, and Graphium's developer receives nothing.
  • Links. Help, About and Welcome links, such as Graphium Support, Report a Bug and this policy, open in your web browser.

AI apps (optional, off until you turn them on)

In Settings > AI Tools you can let AI apps you run yourself, such as Claude, Codex or Cursor, build and change maps with Graphium's map tools. The tools run on your Mac and can reach only the campaign folder you choose there. Graphium sends nothing over the network itself, but whatever the tools return goes to the AI app, and the AI app sends it to its provider (for example Anthropic, OpenAI or Cursor) to answer you. That can include campaign and map names, the maps, token names, DM-only notes, lore files and pictures of a map. Sound folders are separate: an AI app sees a sound folder's name and its sounds' names, tags and notes only for folders you share with it in the same settings, each off until you turn it on.

Agent window (direct download only). Settings > AI Tools > Chat with an agent in Graphium adds Window > Agent, which runs Claude Code or Codex on your Mac with your own account. Before the first message to a provider, Graphium asks you to allow it. Then what you type, what the map tools return, and a short note Graphium adds (the campaign's name and the name and id of the map you are looking at) go to Anthropic or OpenAI. Where the campaign is on your Mac is not sent.

Providers keep what they receive under their own privacy policies and retention terms; Graphium can't delete it. To stop: turn off Let AI apps build maps (and the Agent window) in Settings > AI Tools, turn off any sound folder you shared, and remove Graphium from the AI app's settings. What these features leave on your Mac: the helper link in ~/Library/Application Support/Graphium/bin (removed when you turn AI Tools off), undo copies in ~/Library/Caches/Graphium MCP (deleted after 14 days), and any conversation history Claude Code or Codex keeps in its own folders. Delete those folders to remove them sooner.

Diagnostics and support

Graphium keeps small rolling logs in ~/Library/Logs/Graphium, readable only by your Mac account. They record what happened, with counts and timings, and name a campaign only by a short tag, never its name, folder or contents. Help > Collect Diagnostics puts the logs, macOS version and Mac model in a folder you review in Finder; macOS's own crash reports are added only if you choose Add Crash Reports. Folders outside the system's own are written as <path>. Nothing is sent: you choose whether to make a zip and attach it to a bug report. Reports are deleted after 30 days, or delete them yourself whenever you like. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in macOS settings, Apple handles that under Apple's own privacy policy.

Bug reports and questions go to Graphium's public issue tracker on GitHub, under GitHub's privacy statement. Issues are public, so only attach what you are happy for anyone to see. To remove something you shared, edit or delete your comment, or ask in the issue and it will be deleted.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, this page will say so and show a new date. Questions go to Graphium's issue tracker.
